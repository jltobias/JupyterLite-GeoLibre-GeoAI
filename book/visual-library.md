# Visual library: read, run, watch and compare

Start with a question, not a display type. **Read** opens the Book chapter; **Run** opens its editable notebook; **Watch** plays a 32-second silent storyboard with captions and a transcript. **Infographic** and **Cartoon** open full-size teaching illustrations. All 18 lessons share these routes and the same canonical notebook sources.

## What works without running Python?

The Book includes authored diagrams, saved tables and saved analytical figures. Labs 12–15 use synthetic fixtures; lab 16 uses the bundled report-derived CSV; lab 17 is a discussion workshop. The terrain and report chapters also retain interactive Plotly views, which need JavaScript and the Plotly CDN. Static figures and tables provide alternatives. Live GeoLibre widgets, remote layers and fresh calculations require **Run**, a working kernel and network access.

If a notebook in your browser differs from this Book, download your edits before replacing it: JupyterLite keeps a writable browser copy that can outlive a site update. A deployment does not overwrite your work. Use a fresh browser profile to compare the shipped version, or upload the Book's downloaded notebook under a new name alongside the supplied helpers and data.

## Improvements still requiring new evidence

A true observed time-series map needs dated, comparable spatial layers and explicit handling of missing observations. A 2D/3D flood animation needs a validated inundation model, terrain, vertical datum, scenario definitions and uncertainty—not just relative-risk points. The existing PBIX subset cannot establish the world's ten highest-risk cities. These are future analytical additions, not hidden capabilities of the linked demonstrations.
